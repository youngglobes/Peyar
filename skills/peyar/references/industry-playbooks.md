# Industry playbooks (India)

For each industry: the **brand type**, the **category norm**, the methods that tend to work, the traps to avoid, and the likely **TM classes**. Use the norm to decide whether to play inside it or as a contrarian. Examples are well-known public brands, given to illustrate patterns only.

---

### 1. D2C skincare / personal care
- **Type:** search (online) with credence elements (efficacy)
- **Norm:** short English words, ingredient-science or "clean" cues (Mamaearth, Minimalist, Plum, The Derma Co, Sugar, mCaffeine)
- **Works:** real word repurposed, contrarian (Unhype), evocative
- **Traps:** "Derma", "Glow" and "Skin" are crowded and descriptive (s.9); FSSAI/CDSCO claims words ("cure", "whitening")
- **Classes:** 3, 5 (if ayurvedic or medicated), 35

### 2. Packaged food / snacks / beverages
- **Type:** search (shelf) and experience (taste)
- **Norm:** founder or place heritage (Haldiram's, Bikaji), nostalgic or evocative (Paper Boat), playful English (Too Yumm)
- **Works:** evocative, regional, alphanumeric (Bira 91)
- **Traps:** FSSAI-defined words (Natural, Fresh, Pure, Traditional); "Healthy" claims
- **Millets and regional grains:** grain names (Ragi, Thinai, Samai, Kambu, Jowar, Bajra, Kodo) are **descriptive (D3)** for this category, so use them only as a sub-line, never as the core mark. The category is crowded with "-Millet"/"Millet-" compounds and with "Grandma/Paati/Nani" nostalgia; a contrarian modern name stands out.
- **Classes:** 29, 30, 32, 35

### 3. Restaurants, cafes, cloud kitchens
- **Type:** experience
- **Norm:** South Indian "Bhavan/Vilas" (Saravana Bhavan); cafe English (Third Wave Coffee, Blue Tokai); puns (Chaayos); numbers (1912-style)
- **Works:** evocative, place, alphanumeric, regional word for the cuisine
- **Traps:** too local if you plan to franchise; puns that need explaining
- **Classes:** 43 (+30 for packaged products)

### 4. Jewellery
- **Type:** credence (purity, trust)
- **Norm:** family names and heritage (Kalyan, GRT, Malabar Gold, Joyalukkas); Sanskrit premium (Tanishq); modern online (CaratLane, BlueStone)
- **Works:** founder/family name, Sanskrit, place
- **Traps:** playful names erode trust; "Gold" and "Diamond" are descriptive
- **Classes:** 14, 35

### 5. Fashion / apparel / footwear
- **Type:** search (online) and experience (fit)
- **Norm:** English street names (Snitch, The Souled Store, Comet), Hindi irreverence (Bewakoof), heritage craft (FabIndia, Biba)
- **Works:** contrarian, Hinglish, evocative, numbers
- **Traps:** crowded single English words; phonetic clashes with global brands (s.11 well-known marks)
- **Classes:** 25, 18 (bags), 35

### 6. SaaS / B2B tech
- **Type:** search
- **Norm:** English compounds or coinages that travel globally (Freshworks, Chargebee, Postman, BrowserStack, Zoho)
- **Works:** compound, coined, real word repurposed
- **Traps:** regional words that don't travel to the US/EU buyer; `.com` scarcity (consider get-/-hq/.io/.ai); an "-ify" or "-ly" suffix feels dated
- **Classes:** 9, 42, 35

### 7. Fintech / NBFC / insurance
- **Type:** credence
- **Norm:** clean English (CRED, Jupiter, Groww), Sanskrit-tech fusion (Zerodha), "-Pe" suffix after PhonePe (now a crowded cliché)
- **Works:** real word, Sanskrit, compound
- **Traps:** Rule 8B and RBI/SEBI/IRDAI words (Bank, Capital, Securities, Insurance, Mutual Fund) need eligibility; "-Pe"/"-Pay" names are look-alikes
- **Classes:** 36, 9, 42

### 8. Healthcare, hospitals, clinics, dental
- **Type:** credence
- **Norm:** founder or doctor names (Narayana), mythic or classical (Apollo), place (Manipal), friendly chains (Clove Dental, Sabka Dentist)
- **Works:** founder/doctor name, calm real words, Sanskrit; for a single clinic, doctor name + locality is a fine and trusted norm
- **Traps:** NMC and Dental Council ethics bar "Best", "No.1", "Painless", "Guaranteed"; playful or jokey names; "Hospital" or "Institute" for a single chair clinic
- **Single clinic vs future branches:** a doctor's name builds trust fast but ties the brand to one person, which is a problem when associates run branches or the clinic is sold. If 2+ branches are planned, prefer a calm brand word (Tamil/Sanskrit/English) + "Dental" as the descriptor, and put the doctor's name on the board as "Dr X, BDS, MDS". Locality names (for example "RS Puram Dental") can't be trade marked and break when you open a second branch.
- **Classes:** 44, 35 (+5/10 for products)

### 9. Education / edtech / coaching
- **Type:** credence (outcomes)
- **Norm:** founder name (Byju's), Sanskrit (Vedantu), Hinglish (PhysicsWallah), aspiration (Unacademy)
- **Works:** founder-led, Hinglish (for Bharat audiences), Sanskrit
- **Traps:** "University" is restricted (UGC Act); IIT/IIM look-alikes; "Guaranteed selection"
- **Classes:** 41, 9, 16 (books)

### 10. Real estate / construction / interiors
- **Type:** credence
- **Norm:** family or aspirational English (Prestige, Sobha, Lodha, Godrej Properties); interiors use "Studio/Spaces/Living"
- **Works:** founder/family, aspirational real word
- **Traps:** RERA project names must match the registration; "Infra"/"Developers" add nothing
- **Classes:** 36, 37, 42 (design), 20 (furniture)

### 11. Ayurveda / wellness / supplements
- **Type:** credence
- **Norm:** Sanskrit and Himalayan heritage (Patanjali, Himalaya, Kama Ayurveda, Forest Essentials, Dabur)
- **Works:** Sanskrit, nature-evocative, contrarian-modern (to break the saffron cliché)
- **Traps:** Drugs & Magic Remedies Act (no disease-cure implication); AYUSH rules; deity names on consumables
- **Classes:** 5, 3, 30, 35

### 12. Logistics / mobility / quick commerce
- **Type:** search and experience
- **Norm:** short coinages and puns (Zepto, Blinkit, Rapido, Ola, Porter, Delhivery)
- **Works:** coined, pun, short real word
- **Traps:** renames are common (Grofers → Blinkit) when the category shifts; avoid naming the delivery speed ("10min") in the name
- **Classes:** 39, 35, 9

### 13. Dairy / agri / FMCG staples
- **Type:** search and trust
- **Norm:** cooperative acronyms and regional words (Amul, Aavin, Nandini, Milma), farm-modern (Akshayakalpa, Country Delight)
- **Works:** regional word, acronym with backstory, evocative farm
- **Traps:** FSSAI "Pure/Fresh/Farm-fresh" claims; state co-op look-alikes
- **Classes:** 29, 30, 31, 35

### 14. Home & local services (salons, cleaning, repairs, pest control)
- **Type:** experience
- **Norm:** descriptive English with the city (risky for trade mark), aggregator brands (Urban Company, NoBroker)
- **Works:** contrarian, Hinglish, short real word; city as a suffix only, never in the core name
- **Traps:** purely descriptive names can't be trade marked or defended
- **Classes:** 37, 44, 45, 35

### 15. Pet care
- **Type:** search and experience
- **Norm:** playful English (Heads Up For Tails, Supertails)
- **Works:** pun, evocative, playful real word
- **Traps:** long pun names that are hard to say on the phone
- **Classes:** 31 (pet food), 18, 28, 44 (vet), 35

### 16. Professional services (CA, law, consulting, agencies)
- **Type:** credence
- **Norm:** partner surnames (law, CA); one-word studios for agencies (Become)
- **Works:** founder names, real word repurposed (agencies)
- **Traps:** Bar Council advertising rules; ICAI must approve CA firm names, and they are usually partner-surname based
- **Classes:** 45 (legal), 35 (consulting/advertising), 36 (accounting-related finance)

### 17. B2B finance ops (GST, accounting, payables, compliance SaaS)
- **Type:** credence (getting it wrong = penalties), sold like search (demos, comparisons)
- **Norm:** sober English compounds or Sanskrit-tech (ClearTax→Clear, Zoho Books, Tally, Khatabook, Vyapar, RazorpayX); Hindi/Sanskrit words for ledger, balance and account (khata, lekha, hisab, tula) are common
- **Works:** real word repurposed (balance, settle, net, reconcile metaphors), Sanskrit roots (lekha = record, tula = balance), compound
- **Traps:** "Tax", "GST", "Books", "Khata" are descriptive and crowded; "-Pe"/"-Pay" look-alikes; if the product moves money, RBI payment-aggregator rules apply and "Bank"/"Payments" in the name need eligibility; global buyers (SEA/GCC) need it pronounceable without a Hindi context
- **Classes:** 9, 42, 36 (if handling payments), 35

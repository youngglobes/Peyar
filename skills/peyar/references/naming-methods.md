# Naming methods

Generate across **at least 5** of these methods. Each method fails in its own way, so variety protects the shortlist.

| # | Method | How | Indian examples | Strength | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Real word, repurposed** | An everyday word with a fitting metaphor | Become, Plum, Groww (misspelt), Jupiter, Paper Boat | Arrives with meaning; recall is cheap (the Frozen Iris lesson) | Trade marks for common words are crowded; .com is usually taken |
| 2 | **Regional-language word** | A word from a target-region language that carries the brand idea | Aavin (Tamil), Nandini (Kannada), Vedantu (Sanskrit), Peyar | Cultural depth, owns its geography, often free to trade mark | Hard to say outside the region; check the meaning in other Indian languages |
| 3 | **Sanskrit / classical root** | Sanskrit, Tamil Sangam, Persian or Urdu roots | Zerodha (zero + rodha, "barrier"), Tanishq, Amrut | Pan-Indian dignity; good for credence brands | Can feel old or religious; overused in wellness |
| 4 | **Compound / portmanteau** | Join two meaningful parts | Freshworks, Chargebee, BrowserStack, PhonePe, Delhivery | Descriptive but ownable | Long; puns can age badly |
| 5 | **Coined / invented** | A new word built from sound | Zepto, Zomato, Kodak-style coinages | Trade mark and domains free | Must be taught from zero; budget for it |
| 6 | **Founder / family name** | Founder, family or doctor name | Haldiram's, Narayana Health, Kalyan Jewellers | Trust and heritage; credence-friendly | Hard to sell the company later; personal-name trade marks can conflict |
| 7 | **Place-based** | City, river, region, PIN, lat/long | Manipal, Bikaji (Bikaner), 82°E (India's longitude), Malabar Gold | Rooted and authentic | Boxes in expansion; place names are hard to trade mark (s.9) |
| 8 | **Alphanumeric** | Words + numbers with meaning (dial code, year, coordinates) | Bira 91, 82°E, "1912" | Distinctive, often free | Number must mean something; hard to say aloud ("eighty-two-E"?) |
| 9 | **Hinglish / code-mixed** | Hindi or regional + English blend | PhysicsWallah, Sabka Dentist, Bewakoof, NoBroker | Instantly Indian, mass-market warmth | Can feel cheap for premium; doesn't travel |
| 10 | **Contrarian / anti-category** | Name rejects the category's clichés | Unhype (skincare), Alienkind, Minimalist, NoBroker | Stands out in a crowded category | Needs a real point of view behind it |
| 11 | **Acronym with a backstory** | Initials that also form a word | Amul (Anand Milk Union Limited; "amulya" = priceless) | Formal plus memorable | Pure initials (ABC Pvt Ltd) are forgettable; avoid them |
| 12 | **Evocative / emotional** | Names a feeling or outcome | Paper Boat (nostalgia), Sugar, Snitch | Emotional pull | Feeling must match the audience |

## Alphanumeric names: how to build them

A number or letter code only works when it **means something** and is **easy to say aloud**. Always generate some, and pick the pattern that fits the industry:

| Pattern | Meaning source | Examples (India / global) | Fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dial / country code** | +91 = India | Bira 91, 91springboard | Beverages, fashion, co-working, D2C with Indian pride but global reach |
| **Coordinates / longitude** | Where you're from | 82°E (India's standard meridian) | Premium D2C, skincare, travel, hospitality |
| **Count / quantity** | Product promise | 99acres, 1mg, 7-Eleven, 3M | Marketplaces, health, retail, B2B |
| **Founding year / heritage** | Story | "1912"-style cafe, Forever 21 | Cafes, apparel, jewellery, spirits |
| **Pin code / area code** | Local pride | "641"-style Coimbatore, "560"-style Bengaluru names | Local food, cafes, streetwear |
| **Model / spec style** | Tech, precision | X1, A4, Zee5, M2 | SaaS, hardware, EV, auto, gadgets |
| **Letter + number shorthand** | Sound play | 7UP, 2gether, 4Sale | Beverages, youth brands (use sparingly; can look cheap) |

**Rules:**
- Test how it is **said aloud**: is "91" "ninety-one" or "nine-one"? Pick one and use it consistently.
- Domains: decide between the digits and the spelt-out word, and register both if you can (bira91.com and bira91.in).
- Trade marks: numbers alone are weak marks, so pair them with a word (Bira **91**, not just 91).
- It ages well if the number is a fact (coordinates, dial code). Avoid trend numbers (version 2.0, "10min").

## Global-neutral names

When the brand may sell outside India, or the founder wants a name that doesn't need an Indian context:
- Prefer **real English words used as metaphors** (Plum, Groww, Jupiter, Postman) and **coinages built from sound** (Zepto, Zoho).
- Run the export check (`indian-languages.md` §4) on every global-neutral finalist.
- A global-neutral name still has to pass the Indian meaning screen. It will be said in India first.

## Sound levers (hypotheses, not laws)

- **Open vowels** (a, o, aa, oo) feel lighter, softer and more premium. **Hard stops** (k, t, p, z) feel energetic and techy (Zepto, Kotak).
- **Fewer syllables means better recall.** "Nuke" is one syllable.
- **Repeat sounds** (Chai Point, Tata, Coca-Cola) aid memory.
- **Ending in a vowel** (Ola, Swiggy, Byju's) sounds natural in most Indian languages, which add vowel endings anyway.

## Renaming is expensive

"Name is your perennial ad." Renaming costs you legally, operationally and emotionally. Indian renames (Grofers → Blinkit, UrbanClap → Urban Company, Foodiebay → Zomato, Frozen Iris → Become) each cost years of equity. Spend weeks now to avoid that.

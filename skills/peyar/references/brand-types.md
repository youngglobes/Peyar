# Brand types and how much the name has to carry

Harish Venkatesh sorts brands into three buckets, borrowing from the economics of search, experience and credence goods. The bucket tells you what job the name has to do.

| Type | What it means | Indian examples | The name's job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Search** | Buyers can judge it fully **before** buying, so they research and compare | Electronics, sneakers, appliances, SaaS tools, packaged food on a shelf | **Heaviest load.** Name must be findable, spellable and memorable, because it competes in search results, marketplaces and shelves. "Before I buy you, I will study you." |
| **Experience** | Quality is only known **after** using it, and varies visit to visit | Restaurants, cafes, spas, cinemas (Satyam Cinemas), salons, hotels, studios | Experience becomes the brand over time, so the name must be **easy to recommend**: say it once to a friend and they find you. |
| **Credence** | Even after using it, the buyer **can't fully judge** quality | Hospitals (Apollo), dental clinics, colleges, CAs, lawyers, insurance, supplements | Name must **signal trust**: heritage, founder or doctor name, institution-sounding, calm. Playful names cost trust here. |

## Category-norm analysis (do this before generating)

1. List 8–10 names in the category, both the leaders and new entrants.
2. Note the pattern in each: language (English, Sanskrit, regional), structure (founder name, place, compound, coined, alphanumeric), tone, length.
3. Write the **norm** in one sentence. Example: "Indian D2C skincare = short English words or ingredient-science words (Minimalist, The Derma Co, Plum)."
4. Decide:
   - **Play inside the norm**: instantly legible as the category. Safer, but harder to stand out.
   - **Play outside the norm (contrarian)**: Alienkind (Bangalore) and Unhype in skincare break the pattern on purpose. You stand out, but it needs a clear story.
5. Watch for **number norms**. "1912" reads as a cafe, clothing label or heritage brand, not an IT company. Alphanumeric names (82°E, Bira 91) are a growing pattern, partly because good words are taken.

## No rules, only hypotheses

From the video: "In brand and marketing there is nothing called a rule. Everything is a guideline or a hypothesis." Treat every heuristic in Peyar the same way: as a hypothesis, which a market test (checklist F5) confirms or kills.

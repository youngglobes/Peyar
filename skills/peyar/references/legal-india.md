# Legal checks for brand names in India

> **Not legal advice.** Peyar flags obvious risks. Before you file or spend money, get a registered Trade Marks Agent or IP lawyer to run a full search. Rules change, so check the current text on the official sites below.

"If you did not do a trademark check… someone can sue you." (from the video)

## 1. Trade mark (Trade Marks Act, 1999)

**Where to search (official):**
- Public search: https://tmrsearch.ipindia.gov.in/tmrpublicsearch/ (one class at a time; has a captcha)
- e-Register / status: https://tmrsearch.ipindia.gov.in/eregister/
- Classes (Nice Classification, 45 classes): https://www.wipo.int/classifications/nice/

**How to search:**
1. Pick your classes. Common ones for Indian startups:
   - 3: cosmetics/skincare
   - 5: pharma/supplements/ayurvedic medicine
   - 9: software/apps
   - 14: jewellery
   - 25: clothing/footwear
   - 29: processed foods/dairy
   - 30: snacks/tea/coffee/bakery
   - 32: beverages
   - 35: retail/advertising/D2C store
   - 36: finance/insurance/real estate
   - 41: education/entertainment
   - 42: SaaS/IT services
   - 43: restaurants/cafes/hotels
   - 44: medical/dental/beauty services
2. Run three searches per class: **Wordmark "Match With"**, **Wordmark "Contains"**, and **Phonetic**.
3. Note any mark that is identical or similar for the same or related goods, and its status (Registered, Objected, Opposed, Abandoned, Refused).

**Refusal grounds to self-screen:**
- **s.9 (absolute)**: devoid of distinctive character; describes kind, quality, purpose, value, geographic origin; customary in the trade; may hurt religious sentiments; scandalous or obscene; prohibited under the Emblems Act.
- **s.11 (relative)**: identical or similar to an earlier mark for the same or similar goods, likely to cause confusion; or takes unfair advantage of a **well-known mark**, which is protected across **all** classes (Tata, Amul, Reliance, Infosys and others).

## 2. Company name (Companies Act 2013, s.4; Companies (Incorporation) Rules 2014)

- **Rule 8: resemblance.** The name must not be identical to, or too near, an existing company or LLP name. Plurals, spacing, punctuation, case, "the", tense changes, phonetic spellings and added words like "India" or "Online" do **not** make a name different. Rule 8 also bars names that attract the **Emblems and Names Act**.
- **Rule 8A: undesirable names.** Among them: names that infringe a registered trade mark, offensive names, names suggesting financial activity (bank, insurance, chit fund, nidhi) without the business, names implying a link to a foreign government, national heroes, or the government.
- **Rule 8B: words needing Central Government approval**, including: Board, Commission, Authority, Undertaking, National, Union, Central, Federal, Republic, President, Rashtrapati, Small Scale Industries, Khadi & Village Industries, Financial Corporation, Municipal, Panchayat, Development Authority, Prime Minister, Chief Minister, Minister, Nation, Forest Corporation, Statute, Statutory, Court, Judiciary, Governor, Bureau. Regulated words (Bank, Banking, Insurance, Stock Exchange, Venture Capital, Asset Management, Mutual Fund, Nidhi) need the **regulator's NOC** (RBI, SEBI, IRDAI).
- **Check availability:** MCA "Check Company/LLP Name" at https://www.mca.gov.in/ (MCA Services → Check Company Name), then reserve the name via **SPICe+ Part A** or **RUN**.
- Verify against the current consolidated rules on mca.gov.in. They were amended in 2019 and again in later years.

## 3. Emblems and Names (Prevention of Improper Use) Act, 1950

Bars trade or professional use of names and emblems in its Schedule without permission. The Schedule includes, among others: the UN, WHO and other international bodies; the Indian national flag and the emblem/seal of the Government of India or any state; the words and names "Gandhi", "Nehru", "Shivaji", the Prime Minister and similar; and certain religious-organisation emblems. The government can amend the Schedule by notification. **Read the current Schedule text before calling it.** Entries are about the specific person, emblem or body (and often their pictorial representation), so a common surname used without invoking the leader is a grey area: WARN and verify, not automatic FAIL. Using the name in the company name also triggers MCA Rule 8, which rejects names that attract this Act.
Full text: https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/1896

## 4. Sector rules that affect names

| Sector | Watch for |
|---|---|
| Banking / NBFC / fintech | "Bank", "Finance", "Capital" with lending implications: RBI rules plus Rule 8B |
| Securities / investment | "Securities", "Stock", "Mutual Fund", "Asset Management": SEBI |
| Insurance | "Insurance", "Assurance", "Cover": IRDAI |
| Medical / dental | NMC (Registered Medical Practitioner Professional Conduct) Regulations and the Dental Council of India (Revised Dentists) Code of Ethics Regulations, 2014 limit self-promotion and solicitation. Avoid "Best", "No.1", "Guaranteed", "Painless", "Specialist" (unless qualified), and "Hospital" or "Institute" for a small clinic. Name boards typically show the doctor's name, qualifications and registration number. Confirm the current rules with the State Dental or Medical Council |
| Payments | Products that hold or move money fall under RBI payment-aggregator / payment-system rules; "Bank", "Payments Bank" and "Wallet" names imply licences |
| Food | FSSAI labelling rules ban misleading names: "Natural", "Fresh", "Pure", "Original", "Traditional" have defined conditions under FSS (Advertising & Claims) Regulations 2018 |
| Ayurveda / AYUSH | Drugs & Magic Remedies Act prohibits names implying a cure for listed diseases |
| Legal | Bar Council rules prohibit advocates from advertising; firm names are conservative by norm |
| Education | "University" is restricted (UGC Act s.23); "IIT"/"IIM"-like names are risky |

## 5. GST trade name and domains

- A GST registration can carry a trade name. Check for local conflicts with a Google Maps and GST search in your city.
- Domain disputes: the .IN Dispute Resolution Policy (INDRP) lets trade mark owners take back domains, so a free domain does not mean the name is safe.

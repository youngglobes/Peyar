# Peyar run: Kerala Ayurvedic stress and sleep supplements and teas

## Assumptions
- Brand sells caffeine-free herbal teas and supplement capsules/powders for stress and sleep. Made in Kerala, sold D2C on Amazon and an own site. GCC and UK exports follow later (the 5-year geography is India, GCC, UK).
- Price tier is premium. Audience is urban professionals aged 28-45, mostly English-speaking, many with a Malayali or South Indian link, and a large GCC diaspora.
- Tone is calm, credible and modern, not saffron-cliche. Malayalam or Sanskrit is welcome.
- A .com is strongly preferred because of exports, but is not a hard constraint. A modifier .com (name + herbs/botanicals) is acceptable.
- Founder name is not required. No numerology constraint given. The founder likes "CureVeda", so it is evaluated separately below.
- Likely TM classes: 5 (supplements, ayurvedic preparations), 30 (herbal tea), 35 (online retail). Class 3 later if skincare is added.

## Naming brief
- **Brand type:** credence (even after use, a buyer cannot judge a sleep or stress supplement), sold like search on Amazon (compared and searched). The name must signal calm and trust, and be findable.
- **Category norm:** Indian ayurveda and wellness names are Sanskrit or Himalayan heritage words, a "-veda/-ayur" suffix, or a founder or legacy name (Patanjali, Himalaya, Kerala Ayurveda, Kairali, Organic India, Maharishi Ayurveda, Planet Ayurveda). Newer D2C names are English-science or "adaptogen" words (Wellbeing Nutrition, The Indian Chai). Norm scan used live search results: Kerala Ayurveda, Kairali, Pankajakasthuri, Sitaram Ayurveda, Shesha Ayurveda, Organic India, Maharishi Ayurveda, Planet Ayurveda, Wellbeing Nutrition, Dr. Satnam's Arogyam, Cureveda.
- **Decision:** contrarian-modern. Skip "-veda/-ayur" and Sanskrit heritage clichés. Use a single soft Malayalam word that names a feeling (shade, moonlight, silence), so it reads as a lifestyle brand and is less likely to be confused with the many "-Veda" marks.
- **Feel and sound:** 2-3 syllables, open vowels, no "cure/heal/sleep-disorder" words (Drugs & Magic Remedies Act, UK/EU/GCC claim rules). Must be sayable by a Londoner and a Dubai-based Malayali.
- **No-go zones:** cure/heal/rx words, disease names, deity names, "-veda" and "-ayur" compounds, bare Kerala place names (descriptive and boxing).

## Survivors
Cuts, each with evidence (search results, script output or a stated meaning), in the order the skill requires.

**Cut at step 1 (meaning/slang):**
- Kulir: Tamil/Malayalam "cold, chill"; "kulir jwaram" is chill-fever. Wrong association for a wellness product. Medium confidence.
- Kalmi: Hindi/Bengali "kalmi" is a mango graft or water-spinach (kalmi shak). Reads as food. High confidence.
- Shama: Urdu "flame/candle", Sanskrit "calm". Two readings, and sh/s accent trap. Medium confidence.
- Somara: reads as "soma", the ritual intoxicant in Vedic texts. Low confidence, and a risk for a calm brand.
- Kayal and Munnar and Wayanad: geographic words for Kerala backwaters and towns, so TM s.9 geographic-descriptive and they box the brand to Kerala. Stated meaning.
- Hushleaf, Eventide and Tidewater: 4 syllables or 8-9 letters per score.py (sound 74-92). Soft cut on length, not on conflict.

**Cut at step 2 (descriptive, D3):**
- Nidora, Nidra-family coinages: "nidra" is Sanskrit for sleep, so they are descriptive of the product benefit. Stated meaning.
- Ojas: standard ayurveda term for vitality, crowded and descriptive in the category.

**Cut at step 3 (existing use):**
- Prashama: prashama.com is live as "Prashama Svastha, Mental Health Platform" (check_name.py), a stress and mind-wellness neighbour. Search also surfaced a "Prasham" sleep-inducing ayurvedic tablet (Ask Ayurveda). Same-category risk.

## Founder's name: CureVeda

**Verdict: do not use it.** It fails on a live same-category brand, on the claim word "Cure", and on the "-Veda" cliche.

Evidence:
- **Existing use (D1, E1, E2, E6):** A same-category brand called Cureveda already trades. Web search shows it as an herbal and Ayurveda-inspired supplement brand selling supplements, teas, skincare and stress and heart products, sold through Cureka, HyugaLife and Distacart. YourStory says it launched in 2019 as Cureveda Organic Private Limited, based in Nagpur. A CB Insights record gives a conflicting Maryland address, which I could not resolve. cureveda.com is live as "Cureveda | Premium Herbal Supplements for Natural..." and cureveda.in is live. Existing same-category use is a trade mark and passing-off risk by itself, and it would also take over Amazon search results.
- **Sector claim words (D6):** "Cure" in a name for supplements, tea or ayurveda is an always-FAIL under this checklist. The Drugs & Magic Remedies Act, and UK, EU and GCC claim rules, all restrict names that imply a cure. It also hurts the planned exports.
- **Category norm (A2, A3):** "-Veda" is the exact saffron-cliche the brief rejects, and many brands already use it.
- **Sound (score.py):** 82, with 4 syllables and a v/w accent trap.
- **Meaning and fit:** Veda is a sacred text, a mild religious flag on consumables (C3). The name does not say Kerala, calm, or sleep.

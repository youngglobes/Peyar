# Naming report: lightweight daily-wear gold and diamond jewellery, Madurai to pan-India

## Assumptions
- 18K/14K lightweight gold and diamond pieces for everyday wear, priced mid-premium (roughly Rs 5,000 to Rs 60,000), sold online and in 2 stores.
- Buyers are working women 25 to 40 in metros and tier-2 cities across India. Tamil Nadu is home turf, but the 5-year plan is pan-India.
- Tone is modern, trustworthy and warm. Tamil or Sanskrit roots are welcome. Nothing is ruled out except caste, deity and "claim" words.
- A .com is nice to have, not required. A founder-name format is not wanted. Numerology is not applied.
- Trade mark classes: 14 (jewellery) and 35 (online and retail store services).

## Naming brief
- **Brand type:** credence. Buyers cannot judge purity, hallmark or diamond grade by looking, so the name must sound calm and trustworthy. Because it sells online, it also has to be easy to type and spell (search).
- **Category norm:** Indian jewellery names are family or heritage names (Kalyan, GRT, Jos Alukkas, Thangamayil, Bhima), Sanskrit premium names (Tanishq), or short modern digital names (CaratLane, Melorra, Giva, Mia by Tanishq, BlueStone). Madurai's branded shops are almost all heritage names (Thangamayil, Jos Alukkas, GRT, Kalyan, Malabar, Bhima, Lalitha, Sree Kumaran Thanga Maligai). Search results show CaratLane, Melorra and Giva already own the "lightweight daily wear" idea online.
- **Inside or contrarian:** inside the norm on trust (one calm Tamil or Sanskrit word, easy to say). Contrarian only against the heritage-shop norm: a short, modern, light name instead of "X Jewellers".
- **Feel:** light, glowing, warm, feminine without being "pretty". Open vowels. Two syllables. It should work on a small stud box and on a Madurai shop board.
- **No-go zones:** the plain words for gold (thangam, ponn, sorna, kanak, suvarna, swarn), "Jewellers/Gold/House" as the core, deity names, caste words, and claim words (best, premium, pure).

## Survivors

**Cuts, in screen order, with evidence**
- **Meaning/slang:** Varna cut because it is also the caste-order word in Sanskrit and Hindi (C3 caste marker, high confidence). Laghu cut because it is the plain word for "light" and in Hindi it begins the toilet euphemism "laghu shanka" (medium confidence, and also descriptive).
- **Descriptive (D3):** Sorna cut because it is Tamil for gold. Nithyapon and the "-pon" half of Mellipon sit close to this line; Mellipon is kept as a coined-compound test only.
- **Existing use (web search):**
  - Aurelle: an existing contemporary jewellery brand (Aurelle by Leshna Shah, Renaissance Jewelry, Bandra Mumbai). Cut.
  - Nitya: Nitya Jewels (Mumbai, gold and diamond, since 2020) and a "Nitya" line at GIVA. Cut. Nithyam goes with it as too close.
  - Mullai: Sri Mullai Jewellery, Erode, Tamil Nadu (IndiaMART listing). Cut.
  - Weekday: an H&M Group fashion brand. Cut (well-known mark, TM Act s.11).
  - Lumora: lumora.com and lumora.in are both live sites held by others, and results are crowded with "Lumen/Luxora" jewellery names. Cut.
  - Vaigai: vaigai.com (travel) and vaigai.co.in (transport) are live, and it is a very common Madurai business name (hard to own). Cut.
  - Thuli: sounds like the Marathi "thushi" necklace that fills jewellery search results (confusable, B6). Cut.
  - Kiran, Tejas, Alli, Oli, Aarna, Anvi, Nilaa, Malli, Minnal, Ilai, Tholi, Mutham, Kanmani, Lavanya, Nine to Five, Lightwell: not carried forward. Each is a very common given name, product or film name, or has a weak link to jewellery. No separate evidence was gathered for these, so they are taste cuts, not evidence cuts, and are listed here only so the founder can see the full set considered.

**Fit-gate rejections**
- Kuyil: nothing connects a bird to jewellery, and it already belongs to a radio station.
- Mizhi: the "zh" sound makes it hard for the Delhi, Mumbai or Bengaluru buyer to say without a hint.
- Ezhil: same "zh" problem, and it reads as a male name.
- Mellipon: an invented Tamil phrase that a native speaker may find unnatural, and "pon" is the word for gold.

Finalists: Aabha, Sudar, Deepti, Thendral, Koodal.

## Founder's name: National Gold House

**Verdict: do not use it.** It fails two legal checks and scores 40/100 on sound.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Sound score (score.py) | 40/100: 6 syllables, 17 letters, 3 words that people will shorten |
| Checklist (score36.py) | 65 (5 unverified), **OUT: D-group FAIL on D3 and D4** (also B1, F2) |
| Meaning screen | Clean in the 11 languages checked (high confidence), but "National" is a claim word and "House" adds nothing |
| Existing use | Web search found no jeweller called National Gold House. Weak evidence, since small Indian shops rarely show up in search |
| Domains (check_name.py) | nationalgoldhouse.com, .in, .co.in free; YouTube and X handles free. A strength, not enough to outweigh the rest |

Why it fails:
- **D4, MCA Rule 8B:** "National" is on the list of words that need Central Government approval before a company can be registered under it. A founder using it should expect a name rejection or a long approval process.
- **D3, TM Act s.9:** "Gold House" describes the product and where it is sold. A trade mark agent will likely object, and any registration would be narrow and easy to copy.
- **Positioning:** it sounds like a 1980s heritage jeweller or a gold-loan shop. It does not say light, daily or modern, so it works against what your 25-40 working-woman audience wants. It also promises a national footprint you do not yet have.
- **Execution:** 17 letters is too long for an app icon, a WhatsApp Business display name or an SMS sender ID. It also needs a short form (NGH), which has no meaning.

What the founder likes about it can be kept: weight and trust. Aabha, Sudar or Deepti carry trust through a calm single word, and "Fine Jewellery" in the descriptor line does the rest.

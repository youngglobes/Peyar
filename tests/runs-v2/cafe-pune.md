## Assumptions

- Pune now, about 10 outlets across Maharashtra in 3 years, franchise possible later. So the name must work in Mumbai, Nagpur and Nashik, and for a franchisee in Hindi-belt towns.
- Audience: college students and young office goers, so Gen Z Hinglish and Marathi both feel native.

- A .com is nice, not required. Founder-name format is not wanted. Founder already likes "Gandhi Chai Adda" and it is evaluated in its own section.

- Likely Nice classes: 43 (cafe), 30 (packaged tea and snacks if you sell them), 35 (franchise and retail services).

## Naming brief
**Brand type:** experience (a cafe). The name must be easy to say to a friend once and find again, and it must work on a signboard, in Zomato and on Google Maps.

**Category norm (from web search, Nov 2026):**
- National chains: Chai Point, Chaayos, Chai Sutta Bar, Chai Garam, Chai Vaai Cafe, Chai Darbar.
- Pune names: Yewale Amruttulya, The Chai Cafe, Chai Tapri (Baner), Chai Katta (Bavdhan), Chai Shai - The Teafe, The Chai Bar & Cafe, Chai Chaska.
- Marathi "Chaha" names in Maharashtra franchising: Premacha Chaha, Maitricha Chaha, Chaha House.
- The norm in one sentence: **"Chai" plus an English or Hindi word (Point, Sutta Bar, Tapri, Katta, Cafe), or a pun on chai (Chaayos), with a few Marathi "Chaha" names and founder/heritage names (Amruttulya).**
- Because "Chai + word" is the norm, the generic "Chai Tapri", "Chai Katta" and "Chai Cafe" slots are taken or descriptive.

**Position:** play half inside the norm. Keep "chai" in the name so the category is obvious on a signboard. Make the second word carry the attitude, a Hindi/Marathi suffix or slang a college kid actually says. Pure contrarian names (no "chai") are kept as a second route.
- **Feel:** swagger, friends, "my gang's spot". Not nostalgic, not premium.
- **Sound:** 2-3 syllables, open vowels with one hard stop for energy.
- **No-go zones:** Mumbai-only slang (a Pune brand must not feel imported); "Katta" (gun slang in Hindi, see meaning screen); generic Tapri/Chaha/Cutting/Matka words as the core; any political or national figure.

# Peyar run: Delhi Gen-Z streetwear and sneakers (D2C)

## Assumptions
- Apparel plus sneakers, sold D2C on Instagram and your own site. Delhi start, pan-India in 5 years, Dubai "maybe later".
- Price ₹1,200 to ₹4,000 (mid), buyers 18 to 28, North India first. Tone: bold, irreverent, desi-cool.
- Hinglish and Punjabi welcome. A .com is nice, not required. A founder-name format is not wanted. No numerology constraint.
- TM classes to search: 25 (apparel, footwear), 18 (bags), 35 (online retail).
- IP India, MCA and Instagram were not checked (no live access). They are marked UNVERIFIED throughout.

## Naming brief
- **Brand type:** search (online buyers compare before buying), with an experience layer (fit). The name must be easy to spell, type and say over a noisy Instagram DM.
- **Category norm** (scan: Snitch, Bewakoof, The Souled Store, Comet, Gully Labs, Thaely, Neemans, Crepdog Crew, Jaywalking, NorBlack NorWhite, Almost Gods, Delhiwear, Galli, Farak, WearDuds). Indian streetwear runs on short English words (Snitch, Comet, Galli), Hinglish irreverence (Bewakoof, Gadbadi), or art/craft-flavoured coinages (Jaywalking, Huemn). Very few use a plain Punjabi or Delhi-slang attitude word as the mark.
- **Decision:** play inside the Hinglish norm but contrarian on register. Use a Delhi/Punjabi street word that means attitude or swagger, so it reads as native Delhi rather than a Mumbai-coded joke or a global-brand echo.
- **Feel and sound:** short (1 to 2 syllables), hard consonants for energy (dh, t, k), one clean spelling, easy to say in Dubai.
- **No-go zones:** anything echoing Adidas, Nike, Puma, Snitch or Bewakoof. Caste markers. Deity names on footwear. Vulgar slang. Mumbai slang (Bindaas, Jhakaas).

## Founder's name: Adidesi
**Verdict: do not use it.** It is a near-copy of ADIDAS in the exact class you sell in, and the checklist drops it on two FAILs (D2 well-known mark, B6 homophone) plus A2.

**What the name is:** Adidas with "desi" swapped in for the last syllable. A clever idea for Instagram captions, a weak one for a trade mark.

**Sound score:** 85/100 (4 syllables, flagged as harder to remember). The script scores sound only.

**Meaning screen**

|---|---|---|---|
| Adidesi | Hindi, Urdu, Punjabi, Marathi, Gujarati, Bengali, English | "Desi" is fine and positive for the audience. Joke is an Adidas parody | High |
| Adidesi | Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam | "Adi" = hit/beat or "hey (to a woman)" in Tamil, "that" in Telugu; reads as "Adi Desi" with odd meaning | Medium |
| Adidesi | Arabic (Dubai) | No meaning, but Adidas is one of the strongest brands in the GCC | Medium |

I can't vouch for Odia.

**Existing use and legal evidence**
- Search for "Adidesi brand streetwear sneakers" returned only Adidas pages. Search engines are treating it as a misspelling of Adidas, which means you would sit under Adidas on page 1.
- Delhi High Court has repeatedly enforced ADIDAS against look-alikes: *Adidas AG v. Keshav Tulsiani* (2024, permanent injunction plus damages of about ₹14 lakh), a textile firm that tried a Sindhi word-split defence "Adi-Das" (rejected), and a 2025 order against counterfeit socks restraining use of "deceptively similar marks". The three stripes are described by courts as very well known (TM Act s.11 territory).
- Those cases involved the ADIDAS word or stripes themselves, not "Adidesi" specifically. I found no case on "Adidesi", and an IP India search has not been run (UNVERIFIED).
- The brand would also be a Delhi sneaker brand in class 25, selling directly beside Adidas listings on Instagram and marketplaces. That is the most exposed position possible.

## Next steps
1. Native-speaker check: top 3 names to 1 person per target language (Hindi, Punjabi, Tamil, Arabic-speaking Dubai contact).
2. 5-second recall test with 10 to 20 target buyers: show each name for 5 seconds, talk about something else for 2 minutes, then ask "Which names do you remember? Spell one. What do you think it sells?" Recall + correct spelling + right category guess wins.
3. IP India search in classes 25, 18, 35 (wordmark match, contains, phonetic) → TM agent's opinion → file TM-A.
4. Reserve the company name on MCA (SPICe+ Part A / RUN).
5. Buy domains and claim handles the same day. For Tashan and Dhaak, enquire about the held `.com`/`.in` now; open the Instagram and X handles by hand first.

Expect weeks, not days. Unhype took a studio about 3 months (strategy → shortlist → market test → TM → multi-geography → stakeholder sign-off).

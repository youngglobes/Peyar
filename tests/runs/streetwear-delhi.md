# Peyar test run: Delhi Gen-Z streetwear and sneakers

## Assumptions
- Brief was complete, so no questions were asked.
- Product: streetwear (tees, hoodies, jackets) plus sneakers. Buyers: Gen Z and young millennials, metro and tier-2 India. Sold D2C on Instagram and own site (marketplaces later).
- Start: Delhi. 5-year: pan-India, maybe Dubai (GCC, large Hindi/Punjabi/Urdu-speaking diaspora).
- Price ₹1,200-4,000 (mid). Tone: bold, irreverent, desi-cool. Hinglish and Punjabi welcome. No must-avoid words given.
- Domains: .com is **not** stated as a hard constraint, so I treated it as nice-to-have and did not use the step 4.5 domain filter. Founder name not wanted. No numerology.
- Trade mark classes: 25 (clothing, footwear), 35 (online retail), 18 (bags, if added later).

## Naming brief
**Brand type:** search (buyers compare looks, price and reviews online before buying; fit adds an experience element). The name must be spellable, searchable on Instagram and Google, and shareable in a DM.

**Category norm (scan, live web search, results thin):** Indian streetwear and sneakers cluster into (a) Hindi/Hinglish street words and place words: Gully Labs (Delhi, Hindi in logo), Galli / Galliwear, Delhiwear, Bewakoof, Bonkers Corner; (b) short English or coined words: Comet, Farak, Six5Six (Delhi, numeric), Almost Gods (Delhi), Huemn, Jaywalking, Toffle, Sorta, Thaely, CHK, Neeman's; (c) retailer-style compounds: VegNonVeg (Delhi), Crepdog Crew (Delhi), Superkicks. Norm in one sentence: **"Indian street brands use a short Hindi street word or a short English/coined word, and increasingly a Delhi/gully reference."** Sources: Entrepreneur India, Inc42, Homegrown, Man's World India, PrivateCircle lists (found via WebSearch). The search tool did not return any hit for most of my candidate names; that is weak evidence only, not proof of free use (see Skill problems in the final reply).

**Inside or contrarian:** inside the Hinglish norm, but pick a word with attitude (a put-down turned badge), not another "gully/galli" name. "Gully", "Galli", "Street" and "Desi" are already taken in this space.

**Naming brief:** One idea: *an irreverent Delhi insider's badge*. Feel: loud, cheeky, street-smart. Sound: 2-3 syllables, hard stops (t, k, d), ends in a vowel. No-go: caste or religious words, deity names on footwear, anything that sounds like Adidas/Nike/Puma, abstract claim words, the words gully/galli/desi.

## Founder's name: Adidesi

**Verdict: DROP. Adidesi fails D2 (well-known mark, Adidas in class 25), and also B6 and E6, even though the sound score and domains look fine.**

The skill says to go straight to step 5 for a user's own name, so this was run through the same deep check.

- `score.py Adidesi` -> `85  Adidesi  [4 syl, 7 letters, light/open (premium, soft)]` with the flag "4 syllables: harder to remember". The sound score passes (>=60) and says nothing about the problem below, because it measures sound only.

**Why it feels right and why it still has to go:** the pun is the appeal, and it is also the problem. A name that works by echoing Adidas depends on the echo, and the echo is what a trade mark owner objects to. If the founder wants the "desi Adidas" spirit, keep the attitude, drop the phonetic borrowing.

Skill rule: scores <65 or a D-FAIL means offer 5 alternatives. Five that keep the desi-cool spirit without the echo: **Tapori, Jugaadu, Kadak, Dilli6, Sadda Sole** (the top 5 above). If the founder insists on a pun, the nearest safer move is a pun on a *Hindi* word, not on Adidas, for example a "desi + word" with no "Adi-" start; run it through this same check first.

## Next steps
1. Native-speaker check: top 3 names to 1 person per target language (Hindi, Punjabi, Urdu, plus Tamil/Telugu/Malayalam/Bengali if the brand will ship there). Ask specifically: "what does it mean, is it rude, would you wear it?"
2. 5-second recall test with 10-20 target buyers (script below).
3. IP India search in classes 25, 35 (and 18 if bags): Wordmark Match, Contains and Phonetic for each of the top 3 → TM agent's opinion → file TM-A. Also search "Adidas"-style phonetic conflicts only if the founder insists on Adidesi.
4. Reserve the company name on MCA (SPICe+ Part A / RUN).
5. Buy domains and claim handles the same day. Check Instagram by hand first: it is the main sales channel and the script cannot check it.

Expect weeks, not days. Unhype took a studio ~3 months (strategy → shortlist → market test → TM → multi-geography → stakeholder sign-off).

**Recall test script:** show each name for 5 seconds, talk about something else for 2 minutes, then ask: "Which names do you remember? Spell one. What do you think it sells?" Recall + correct spelling + right category guess wins.

*Peyar gives heuristics, not legal advice. Have a registered trade mark agent confirm before filing.*
